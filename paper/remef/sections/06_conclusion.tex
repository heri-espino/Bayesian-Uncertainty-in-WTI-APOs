\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:short_conclusion}

We examine two distinct effects in WTI average-price-option
valuation: changing a volatility estimate and averaging model
prices over uncertainty about that estimate. Posterior integration
affects the conditional \emph{point price} through volatility
posterior dispersion and option-price curvature; the dispersion
of conditional prices induced by the posterior is a different
object. Large synthetic simulations illustrate the mechanism.
In the original October-2026 \emph{expiry} sample, the posterior
integrated and plug-in values are nearly identical despite
appreciably wider posterior distributions of conditional
model prices.

The original forward-in-time Barchart/LO comparison finds much
smaller pricing errors when the volatility input comes from
earlier option prices rather than the tested historical-return
models. An independent longer Databento AAO settlement-mark
exercise also favors earlier AAO implied volatility, although
its pricing function and information set differ. Within
that reduced-form exercise, introducing a Bayesian model for
earlier implied-volatility innovations does not reliably improve
on carrying forward the latest implied volatility. Additional
Bayesian modeling of historical settlement-mark residuals
does not maintain nominal interval coverage during early 2026.

The practical recommendation is to compare Bayesian
posterior-integrated option values with a matched plug-in price,
benchmark the volatility input against an honestly lagged
option-implied alternative, and report \emph{model-price posterior
dispersion} separately from empirical forecast-error coverage.
These comparisons should be interpreted relative to the
specific market observations and pricing assumptions, not as
a general ranking of stochastic-volatility models.

The evidence does not establish that the reduced-form
terminal-average approximation or the maintained one-factor
futures dynamics are structurally correct. End-of-day
settlement fields can be model-generated, and the older
independent-date market comparisons remain small. Additional
work with independently time-stamped CL fixing histories,
market quotes and carefully designed hedging tests is needed
before attributing pricing differences to tradable market
inefficiencies or to a variance risk premium.
