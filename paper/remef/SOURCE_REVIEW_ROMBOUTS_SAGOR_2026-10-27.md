## 2. Rombouts & Stentoft **2014 published**: checked claims and limit

The **published article abstract** says the researchers perform **classical and Bayesian estimation on the same mixed-normal option model**, compare maximum-likelihood estimates with posterior moments, and find similar option-pricing errors, with a **minor incremental impact attributed to parameter uncertainty**. The ESSEC abstract further conditions this conclusion on **large amounts of return data** and notes that Bayesian methods could matter with **substantially smaller estimation samples**. See [Elsevier publisher](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167947313002454) and [ESSEC record](https://faculty.essec.edu/en/research/712-bayesian-option-pricing-using-mixed-normal-heteroskedasticity-models/).

**Subtle but crucial distinction:** the 2014 abstract confirms the empirical finding for a **Bayesian-versus-classical estimation/pricing comparison**. It does **not**, on its own, establish that their precise estimator was
`E[C(sigma)|D] - C(E[sigma|D])`, that their plug-in was the posterior mean **volatility** under a constant-`sigma` payoff, or that they performed our same controlled `C_PI-C_PM` test. Therefore avoid saying “the paper already proves exactly our PI–PM result.” Instead say: **“The minor effect of parameter uncertainty on option-pricing errors has established empirical precedent, although the underlying options, models and comparison design differ.”**

**Critical remaining scholarly task:** obtain and verify the *complete 2014 journal article* (legally through institutional access or an authorized author version), its tables, estimator definitions, estimation windows, model variants and robustness before closing the 2014 full-text novelty audit. The user-supplied 2009 CIRANO version is **insufficient to mark that step complete**.

**Potential misleading comparison:** Sagor's table 5 includes a column labelled **BSM** and a distinct **posterior price mean**, but this is **not established as** the same `C(E[sigma|D])` plug-in from our fixed price function. Its joint parameter `(H,σ)` posterior and model formula differ. Do **not** use `15.15 versus 15.01` or `7.86 versus 7.83` as a published PI–PM benchmark against our USD 0.000745/bbl. Likewise, their posterior price **widths are not predictive intervals for actual market-mark forecast errors**.

**Sagor verdict:** strongly overlapping Bayesian model-price uncertainty propagation using WTI underlying **returns**, but not a direct WTI **Asian contract and market-mark** replication; it actually strengthens the need for our three-estimand distinction and to avoid mistaking a broad Bayesian framework for a new commodity option pricing theorem.

## 5. Better author-language for REMEF's Introduction (source-bounded)

> Bayesian parameter integration is not a new option-pricing method. Rombouts and Stentoft (2009) show how to use parameter posterior draws to obtain risk-neutral predictive price distributions in mixed-normal heteroskedasticity models. In their subsequent journal article, Rombouts and Stentoft (2014) find that, within their S&P 500 model and abundant-data setting, Bayesian and classical estimation produce similar option-pricing losses and parameter uncertainty has a relatively small incremental effect. More recently, Sagor, Boone, and Ghanam (2026) propagate posterior uncertainty in the Hurst parameter and volatility into **hypothetical European** option-price distributions using WTI and natural-gas returns. These studies establish the importance of distinguishing price-point estimates from parameter-induced price uncertainty, but do not directly measure our narrower combination of (i) posterior-integrated versus posterior-mean **WTI arithmetic-average** option prices, (ii) **strictly lagged** implied-versus-historical volatility information on observed AAO settlement marks, and (iii) conditional model-price bands versus **actual settlement-mark residual** interval coverage. The present analysis assesses these comparisons within specified pricing and information sets; it does not claim a new Bayesian pricing formula or independent validation from executable option trades.

**Use 2014 as the standard bibliography citation** for the final published paper. Add a separate 2009 citation only if the actual 2009-specific algorithm/working-paper result is discussed; otherwise citing both versions could falsely inflate the number of independent studies.

## 6. Reviewer-relevant takeaways and action gates

1. **Originality challenge partly resolved:** the two supplied **full texts** demonstrate meaningful distinctions in payoffs and **what is actually observed**. But broad novelty is reduced; the claim must remain a targeted WTI contract/information-set comparison.
2. **More serious market-data critique now evident:** Rombouts–Stentoft 2009 uses **liquid-volume-filtered midquotes**, whereas Paper A uses price fields that may be mechanically produced by an exchange settlement model. We must not call them comparable independent option-trade verification.
3. **Inference priority:** original 2,381 observations collapse to **15** valuation-date clusters, compared with about 52 weekly benchmark dates in the 2009 paper. No cross-study superiority can be inferred from raw record count; date-block intervals and timing/mark independence are essential.
4. **Information-price mismatch:** physical-return `σ` and option-implied `σ` estimate different economic quantities. Historical-vs-IV price-error differences cannot on their own identify a risk premium or prove Bayesian inference failed.
5. **Point-versus-distribution priority:** keep `C_PI-C_PM`, `SD[C(σ)|D]`, and market-mark residual coverage as separate reported estimands. Neither prior paper answers all three **in this precise option-mark design**.
6. **No automatic model expansion:** the 2026 paper is not a reason to insert fractional Brownian motion or joint Hurst inference into our fixed-source-effect experiment.
