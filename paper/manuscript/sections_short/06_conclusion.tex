\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:short_conclusion}

Posterior integration and the choice of volatility input affect WTI APO pricing in different ways. The synthetic experiment shows that PI and PM can diverge when the volatility posterior is dispersed and the pricing function has substantial curvature. In the October-2026 WTI sample, however, the PI--PM difference is small even though the posterior distribution of model prices remains much wider. Parameter-induced price dispersion should therefore not be inferred from the difference between two point estimates.

A bounded synthetic check also illustrates that close agreement of PI and PM
can coexist with substantial known-parameter pricing error. Under a hypothetical
change in cross-contract dependence, posterior model-price interval coverage
falls even though the PI--PM difference is unchanged. These coverage
frequencies are provisional pending finer-grid interval-quantile checks.

In the conditional APO pricing comparisons, earlier-date option-implied volatility gives lower errors against settlement-price proxies than the tested historical-volatility benchmarks. The result persists without cross-sectional smile fitting and when implied volatility is obtained from standard monthly WTI futures options. Within the maintained one-factor model and the limited number of valuation dates, the source of volatility information matters more for agreement with settlement-price proxies than posterior integration alone. These comparisons do not establish fully prospective performance or a general advantage over richer commodity models.
