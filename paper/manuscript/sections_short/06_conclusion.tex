\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:short_conclusion}

We examine posterior integration and the choice of volatility input in WTI APO valuation. A second-order approximation links the PI--PM difference to posterior volatility variance and price curvature. The synthetic experiment shows that posterior integration can materially affect option values with short historical samples and pronounced curvature.

In the observed October-2026 sample, PI and PM are nearly identical, although the posterior distribution of model prices remains substantially wider. The effect of integration on the point price should therefore be distinguished from parameter-induced price uncertainty.

For the out-of-sample observations, earlier-date option-implied volatility yields considerably lower pricing errors than the historical-volatility benchmarks, including rolling windows, EWMA, and horizon-matched GARCH. The advantage persists with same-contract lagged implied volatility and in the cross-instrument LO comparison.

These results are conditional on a limited number of valuation dates and the maintained one-factor pricing model. They do not identify a structural physical-versus-risk-neutral volatility difference. Within that scope, the choice of volatility input matters far more for pricing accuracy than the PI--PM distinction.
