\section{Introduction}\label{sec:short_intro}

Option values depend on estimates of volatility and other parameters. One way to account for parameter uncertainty is to average option values over the posterior distribution rather than use a single estimate. This approach is established in Bayesian option pricing and forecasting \citep{guidolin2003,romboutsstentoft2014,guptareisinger2014}. Our first question is simple: when does posterior averaging make an important difference to the option price?

Asian options depend on an average of prices rather than only the final price. Their sensitivity to volatility changes with the strike, time to maturity, and number of prices already fixed. Established pricing methods include geometric-average control variates and conditioning approximations \citep{kemnavorst1990,curran1994}. Commodity applications must also account for futures prices at different maturities. More complex models may include stochastic convenience yields, jumps, or stochastic volatility \citep{schwartz1997,shirayatakahashi2011,ewaldwu2023}. We do not propose a new Asian-option pricing model. We keep one simple pricing model and compare two decisions: averaging over uncertainty in volatility and choosing where the volatility estimate comes from.

These decisions should not be confused. Posterior averaging changes how we use a given volatility estimate and its uncertainty. Choosing historical or option-implied volatility changes the information supplied to the pricing model.

Let \(C^Q(\sigma)\) be the risk-neutral option value conditional on volatility \(\sigma\), let \(\mathcal D\) denote the historical return data, and let \(\bar\sigma=\mathbb E[\sigma\mid\mathcal D]\). The posterior-integrated price and posterior-mean plug-in price are
\begin{equation}
 C_{PI}=\mathbb E[C^Q(\sigma)\mid\mathcal D],
 \qquad
 C_{PM}=C^Q(\bar\sigma).
 \label{eq:short_pi_pm}
\end{equation}
A second-order expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 C_{PI}-C_{PM}
 \approx
 \frac12 C^{Q\prime\prime}(\bar\sigma)
 \operatorname{Var}(\sigma\mid\mathcal D).
 \label{eq:short_taylor}
\end{equation}
The approximation links the size and sign of the PI--PM difference to posterior variance and the curvature of the price with respect to volatility. The spread of model prices follows a different relationship: locally, it grows with the posterior standard deviation of volatility. Thus, PI and PM can be close even when parameter uncertainty leads to a wide range of model prices.

The empirical application uses WTI Average Price Options (APOs), whose payoff depends on the arithmetic average of daily first-nearby WTI futures settlements. We compare historical volatility with implied volatility from both APOs and standard monthly WTI futures options. When pricing an APO, we estimate implied volatility only from earlier option dates. We use the current futures curve, known fixings, and discount factor to calculate its model price. Historical-volatility methods use returns available by the stated cutoff.

We find three main results. First, the synthetic experiment supports Equation~\eqref{eq:short_taylor}. The PI--PM difference can be large with short return histories, strong price curvature, and few known fixings. Second, PI and PM are nearly identical in the October-2026 WTI sample, even though uncertainty about the model price is much larger than their difference. Third, earlier option-implied volatility gives much lower out-of-sample pricing errors than the historical-volatility methods tested. Rolling-window, exponentially weighted, and horizon-matched GARCH(1,1) estimates do not achieve similar pricing errors. Carrying forward a contract's previous implied volatility shows that a fitted smile is not needed for this result. We also find lower errors using implied volatility from standard monthly WTI futures options, rather than APOs.

This is a comparison of pricing methods, not a measurement of the difference between physical and risk-neutral volatility. Implied volatility may also capture risk premia, market features omitted from the model, liquidity, and settlement practices. The findings apply to the data and methods tested.
