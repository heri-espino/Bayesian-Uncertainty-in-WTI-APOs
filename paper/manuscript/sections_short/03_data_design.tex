\section{Data and empirical design}\label{sec:short_design}

\subsection{WTI Average Price Options}

The empirical sample is built from individual Barchart histories for WTI Average Price Options. The raw archive contains 213 unique contracts and 11,179 contract-date observations, with expiry months ranging from September 2026 to June 2029. We compare model prices with the Barchart end-of-day settlement field. Calls and puts are retained as available, and moneyness is recomputed by valuation date rather than assigned once at the contract level.

For each contract-date observation, we identify the first-nearby CL settlements already fixed, the futures contracts that apply to the remaining fixing dates, and the valuation-date futures prices used to initialize those contracts. This preserves the contract feature that the first-nearby futures contract can change during the averaging month.

Historical volatility is estimated from WTI futures returns available by the valuation date. The baseline uses the labelled continuous/front-month \texttt{CL=F} return series as the physical-measure proxy. A robustness check reconstructs a first-nearby return history contract by contract from CL settlements and excludes returns spanning contract switches.

\subsection{Out-of-sample volatility comparisons}

We compare historical and option-implied volatility inputs using out-of-sample APO pricing errors. Implied volatilities are estimated using only option observations from dates earlier than the target date. We then reprice the target APO with the current futures curve, realized fixings, and discount factor. Historical-volatility estimates use returns available under their stated valuation-date cutoff. Seven expiries pass the committed-data coverage requirement, producing 2,381 out-of-sample contract-date observations over 15 target dates.

Our historical-volatility benchmark integrates over the posterior estimated from the expanding return sample. We also test estimates using the most recent 63, 126, or 252 returns and an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) with a 63-business-day half-life. Full results appear in Online Supplement Section S1 and Table S1. We additionally use a Gaussian GARCH(1,1) model. We estimate its parameters from returns strictly earlier than the target date and use the target-date return only to update the end-of-day conditional variance. We then forecast conditional variances over the remaining fixing dates. Finally, we convert these forecasts to a constant annualized volatility that matches the variance of the remaining arithmetic-average component under the maintained one-factor pricing model.

We obtain contract-level APO implied volatilities by inverting the Curran pricing approximation. For each expiry, the previous-day implied-volatility smile is estimated on the latest available earlier date. A second specification fits a smile using all earlier dates, giving more weight to recent observations. Both depend on moneyness and option type. Neither uses the target-date APO settlement to estimate volatility.

We also test whether fitting an implied-volatility smile is necessary. For each contract with an earlier implied volatility, we carry its latest available implied volatility forward and reprice it using the current futures curve, discount factor, and fixing schedule, including realized fixings. This same-contract comparison is available for 2,366 of the 2,381 observations.

Pricing performance is summarized by mean error, mean absolute error (MAE), and root mean squared error (RMSE). Dependence across contracts observed on the same valuation date is preserved with valuation-date cluster bootstrap resampling.

\subsection{Cross-instrument validation}

The APO experiment still obtains implied volatility from the same option family later used as the pricing target. An independent validation therefore uses standard monthly American WTI options on futures (CME product LO) obtained through Databento. Official LO option settlements are matched to CL futures settlements, and contract-level implied volatilities are recovered with a Cox--Ross--Rubinstein futures-option tree.

For each APO target date, only LO observations from earlier dates are used. Implied-volatility smiles are fitted separately for the underlying CL futures contracts needed by the October-2026 APO fixing schedule. The resulting maturity-specific volatilities are mapped into the maintained scalar-volatility APO pricing model. We compare 253 APO contract-date observations over 10 target dates for which both external specifications can be evaluated within the moneyness range observed in earlier LO data. Online Supplement Section S4 and Table S4 report checks of the constant-volatility mapping and the number of steps in the Cox--Ross--Rubinstein tree.

The cross-instrument experiment is not intended to estimate a structural variance risk premium. Its role is narrower: it asks whether the out-of-sample improvement associated with prior option-implied volatility remains when the volatility information comes from a separate WTI option market rather than from earlier APO settlements.
