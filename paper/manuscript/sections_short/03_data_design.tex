\section{Data and valuation design}\label{sec:short_design}

\subsection{Contracts, settlements, and historical returns}
The raw Barchart archive contains 213 unique APO contracts and 11,179 option-date observations, with expiry months from September 2026 through June 2029. These are contract expiries, not the span of observed valuation dates. The pricing target is the end-of-day settlement field. Calls and puts are retained as available, and moneyness is recomputed from the valuation-date expected average rather than assigned once to each contract.

Each valuation reconstructs realized first-nearby CL settlements, the futures maturities applicable to unresolved fixing dates, and the current CL term structure. The October-2026 baseline contains 310 contract-date observations over 12 valuation dates, from August 25 to September 10, 2026. This panel compares PI and PM and reports posterior model-price interval widths.

Historical inference uses an expanding return sample beginning in January 2024. Yahoo \texttt{CL=F} is the baseline continuous/front-month proxy. A source check reconstructs the first-nearby history from individual CL settlements, excludes returns spanning contract switches, and omits returns depending on missing prices. The contractual fixing sequence used for valuation remains separate from the continuous-contract proxy used for inference.

\subsection{Earlier-date volatility inputs and current-state repricing}
The forward comparison covers seven expiries from September 2026 through September 2028, with 2,381 contract-date holdouts over 15 target dates. Option-implied volatility inputs use observations dated strictly before the target date. Repricing uses the target-date futures curve, realized fixings, and discount factor. Historical estimators use returns available by the valuation date. This timing convention evaluates the transfer of earlier volatility information to later-date valuation conditional on the current market state.

The expanding historical PI baseline is compared with rolling estimates from 63, 126, or 252 returns and EWMA with a 63-business-day half-life. These choices were fixed before inspecting their pricing errors. A Gaussian GARCH(1,1) comparator is fitted to earlier returns; the target-date return updates its conditional variance state without re-estimating parameters. Forecast variances over the remaining fixing horizon are mapped to an effective constant volatility by matching the unresolved average's variance under the common-factor model. This is a historical variance-forecast comparator, rather than a full risk-neutral GARCH option model.

APO implied volatilities are recovered by inverting the Curran pricing approximation. A quadratic log-moneyness relation with call/put interactions is fitted either to the latest earlier observation date or to an expanding earlier-date sample with recency weights. Same-day target APO settlements are excluded from estimation. A separate comparator carries forward the latest earlier implied volatility of the same contract and reprices it with the current market state. This persistence comparison is available on 2,366 exact common observations.

Pricing errors are model values minus settlement values. Mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared error (RMSE) are computed on identical rows within each comparison. Bootstrap uncertainty resamples valuation-date clusters, preserving dependence among contracts observed on the same date. Complete historical benchmarks appear in Online Supplement Section S1.

\subsection{Volatility information from vanilla-WTI options}
The external validation uses standard monthly American WTI options on futures, CME product LO, accessed through Databento. Official LO settlements are matched to CL futures settlements and inverted with a 160-step Cox--Ross--Rubinstein futures-option tree. Only earlier-date LO observations enter the target APO volatility input.

Implied-volatility smiles are fitted separately for the futures maturities required by the October-2026 fixing schedule. If $w_u$ is the fraction of remaining fixing dates assigned to maturity $u$, the primary effective volatility is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\sigma^{\mathrm{LO,eff}}_t(K)
 =\left[\sum_u w_u\{\widehat\sigma^{\mathrm{LO}}_{u,<t}(K)\}^2\right]^{1/2}.
 \label{eq:short_lo_transfer}
\end{equation}
The subscript $<t$ denotes strictly earlier option information, evaluated at target-date moneyness. This fixing-count RMS mapping is a scalar transfer rule, not an exact variance identity for the arithmetic average. Section S4 evaluates an alternative that matches the unresolved average's variance under the same common-factor representation.

The main external comparison uses 253 October-2026 APO observations over 10 target dates inside the observed prior LO moneyness support of both external specifications. Moving the volatility source to vanilla options tests whether the option-implied improvement transfers across instruments. It does not make the source and target market environments statistically independent.
