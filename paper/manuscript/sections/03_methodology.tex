\section{Methodology and research design}\label{sec:methodology}\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Physical inference and risk-neutral pricing}

Under the physical measure, historical WTI log returns follow a constant-volatility geometric Brownian motion. For log return \(R_i\) over interval \(\Delta t\),
\begin{equation}
 R_i\sim
 \mathcal N\!\left[
 \left(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2\right)\Delta t,
 \sigma^2\Delta t
 \right].
 \label{eq:return_likelihood}
\end{equation}
The baseline prior is
\begin{equation}
 \mu\sim\mathcal N(0,1),
 \qquad
 \sigma\sim\operatorname{InvGamma}(2,0.1),
 \quad \sigma>0.
 \label{eq:priors}
\end{equation}
We sample the empirical posterior with random-walk Metropolis in \((\mu,\log\sigma)\). In the large synthetic experiment, integrating out \(\mu\) analytically conditional on \(\sigma\) yields a one-dimensional posterior calculation free of MCMC error. The online supplement provides sampler settings, calibration checks, and alternative models.

Valuation is performed separately under the risk-neutral measure. For each unresolved WTI fixing, the maintained futures benchmark is
\begin{equation}
 dF_u=\sigma F_u\,dW_u^Q.
 \label{eq:futures_q}
\end{equation}
Physical drift is excluded from the risk-neutral dynamics. Realized fixings enter deterministically, while remaining fixings evolve from the relevant contracts on the valuation-date CL futures curve. Holding the one-factor dynamics fixed isolates the effect of posterior integration from the choice of volatility input.

We calculate baseline empirical prices by Monte Carlo. For the larger out-of-sample comparisons and checks using constant-volatility inputs, we use Curran's deterministic conditioning approximation \cite{curran1994} under the same one-factor model. Difficult contracts are also checked with high-precision pseudo-random and scrambled-Sobol calculations. Numerical diagnostics are summarized in Section~\ref{sec:robustness} and documented in detail in the supplement.

\subsection{Posterior integration and uncertainty about model prices}

The primary comparison is between
\begin{equation}
 C_{PI}=\mathbb E[C^Q(\sigma)\mid\mathcal D],
 \qquad
 C_{PM}=C^Q(\bar\sigma),
 \qquad
 \bar\sigma=\mathbb E[\sigma\mid\mathcal D].
 \label{eq:estimators}
\end{equation}
A Taylor expansion around \(\bar\sigma\) gives the central mechanism,
\begin{equation}
 C_{PI}-C_{PM}
 \approx
 \frac12 C^{Q\prime\prime}(\bar\sigma)
 \operatorname{Var}(\sigma\mid\mathcal D).
 \label{eq:taylor_gap}
\end{equation}
The leading PI--PM difference is governed by posterior variance and price curvature. By contrast,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{SD}\!\left(C^Q(\sigma)\mid\mathcal D\right)
 \approx
 |C^{Q\prime}(\bar\sigma)|
 \sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(\sigma\mid\mathcal D)},
 \label{eq:price_uncertainty_delta}
\end{equation}
so the posterior standard deviation of model prices scales locally with the posterior standard deviation of volatility, rather than its variance. A small PI--PM difference is therefore compatible with substantial parameter-induced price dispersion.

The repeated-sampling benchmark also reports posterior-mode and maximum-likelihood plug-in prices. Posterior RMS volatility is examined separately as a constant-volatility sensitivity, not as another posterior-integration rule.

\subsection{Synthetic mechanism experiment}

We simulate return histories under \(\mathbb P\) and calculate target option values under \(\mathbb Q\) using the known volatility. The large mechanism map contains 175,000 independent histories and spans short to long estimation samples, annual volatility from 0.10 to 0.80, multiple maturities and moneyness states, and partial-fixing configurations. For each history and contract state, the experiment records the PI--PM gap, posterior variance of volatility, local pricing curvature, and the approximation in Equation~\eqref{eq:taylor_gap}.

The synthetic design tests the mechanism in Equation~\eqref{eq:taylor_gap}; empirical pricing performance against market settlements is assessed separately. The supplement reports the complete simulation grid and replication counts.

\subsection{Forward WTI APO validation}

Historical volatility is estimated from returns available by each valuation date. APO implied volatility is obtained by inverting Curran's approximation against observed option settlements. It is a model-equivalent pricing input rather than a structural risk-neutral volatility estimate.

We estimate implied-volatility smiles only from option dates preceding the pricing date. One specification uses the most recent earlier date; another weights all prior dates toward recent observations. The out-of-sample panel covers 2,381 contract-date observations across seven expiries and 15 target dates.

We compare the option-implied results with several historical-volatility estimates: rolling windows of 63, 126, and 252 returns, an exponentially weighted moving average (EWMA) with a 63-business-day half-life, and a Gaussian GARCH(1,1) forecast matched to the remaining fixing dates. All specifications supply volatility to the same pricing model. To assess the role of smile fitting, we also carry forward each contract's most recent earlier implied volatility and reprice using the valuation-date futures curve, realized fixings, remaining fixing schedule, and discount factor. GARCH estimation and the conversion of variance forecasts to one constant volatility are described in the supplement.

We evaluate pricing errors against end-of-day settlements using mean error, MAE, and RMSE. Bootstrap inference resamples valuation dates to preserve contemporaneous dependence among contracts. Liquidity restrictions based on positive volume and open interest are reported as robustness checks.

\subsection{Independent vanilla-WTI validation}\label{sec:external_lo_method}

The cross-instrument test obtains implied volatility from standard monthly American WTI futures options (CME product LO), rather than APOs. We match LO settlements to the corresponding CL futures settlements and infer volatility with an American Cox--Ross--Rubinstein futures-option tree. The main inversion uses a 160-step tree. The supplement reports checks using different numbers of steps.

LO implied-volatility surfaces are fitted by underlying futures contract using observations strictly before each APO target date, then evaluated at the APO strike. We map maturity-specific LO volatilities to the scalar input required by the APO model. The primary rule uses fixing-count weighted RMS volatility; a sensitivity matches the variance of the remaining arithmetic average under the one-factor assumption. Neither mapping is treated as an exact structural identity.

The external comparison is restricted to the common moneyness support of both prior LO specifications, leaving 253 APO contract-date observations over 10 target dates. No target-date APO settlement enters the LO volatility estimates. This tests whether the out-of-sample advantage transfers across option families.

\subsection{Scope of supporting diagnostics}

Additional checks in the online supplement cover prior and window sensitivity, Student-\(t\) returns, first-nearby return reconstruction, posterior RMS volatility, calibration, numerical accuracy, LO tree refinement, volatility surface shape, and liquidity. Section~\ref{sec:robustness} reports only what these checks establish for the three central findings; implementation settings and complete numerical results are preserved in the online supplement.
