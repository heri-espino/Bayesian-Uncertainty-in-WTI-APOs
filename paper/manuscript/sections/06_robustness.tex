\section{Robustness and extensions}\label{sec:robustness}

The baseline model is intentionally simple enough that the source of any full-Bayes/plugin difference can be identified. The robustness agenda therefore separates statistical sensitivity from commodity-pricing-model sensitivity and, equally importantly, separates parameter-integration effects from benchmark-quality effects.

\subsection{Prior and estimation-window sensitivity}

The inverse-gamma volatility prior in Equation~\eqref{eq:priors} is a baseline rather than a universal recommendation. The main posterior-pricing comparisons should be repeated with weaker and alternative positive-support priors. A result attributed to parameter uncertainty should not be driven by one prior parameterization. Likewise, empirical posteriors should be compared under alternative historical windows. Shorter windows react more quickly to volatility changes but contain less information; longer windows concentrate the posterior while potentially mixing distinct volatility regimes. This trade-off is directly relevant to Equation~\eqref{eq:taylor_gap}.

The implemented empirical runs use multiple MCMC chains and report Gelman--Rubin diagnostics for both $\mu$ and $\sigma$. In the October-2026 positive-volume-date sample, $\hat R_\sigma$ is approximately one across valuation dates and posterior standard deviation for volatility remains close to 0.0112--0.0114. Effective-sample-size diagnostics and alternative prior families remain useful extensions. Because posterior modes depend on parameterization and smoothing choices, the reported marginal sigma-mode plug-in is treated as a conventional comparator rather than a uniquely Bayesian decision rule.

\subsection{Liquidity and settlement quality}

Liquidity is already economically relevant in the first empirical panel. The all-dates October-2026 sample contains 310 contract-date observations, but only six contract-date observations report positive daily volume. Restricting to those six observations lowers full-Bayes RMSE from 0.5083 to 0.1783, while the full-Bayes/posterior-mean difference remains extremely small. This pattern shows why liquidity and benchmark quality must be analyzed separately from the parameter-integration question.

The current results therefore use three explicit sample definitions: all eligible contract-date observations, all contracts observed on dates with at least one positive-volume contract, and only contract-date observations that themselves report positive volume. The latter is the strictest transaction-activity robustness sample, but with $N=6$ it is too small for strong statistical ranking claims. Future data acquisition should prioritize additional actively traded option observations rather than simply adding zero-volume marks.

Contract histories should also be cross-checked against CME daily settlements on a larger set of dates and strikes where public bulletin observations can be matched. Any systematic discrepancy between Barchart's end-of-day field and CME settlement would require redefining the empirical benchmark before stronger model-comparison conclusions are drawn.

\subsection{Alternative moneyness definitions}

The baseline moneyness variable uses the expected final APO average in Equation~\eqref{eq:expected_average}. Robustness checks can compare it with simpler forward-based measures and with normalized intrinsic-distance measures that explicitly condition on the realized portion of the monthly average. The economic conclusions should not depend on a single arbitrary ATM bandwidth.

\subsection{Futures-curve dynamics}

Equation~\eqref{eq:futures_q} is a one-factor benchmark. Crude-oil futures are exposed to term-structure movements that need not be perfectly correlated across maturities. A natural extension replaces the common-volatility representation with a low-dimensional correlated futures-curve model. A further extension allows stochastic volatility or mean reversion. These models can improve absolute fit, but they introduce additional parameters whose uncertainty must also be propagated. They therefore provide a demanding test of whether the qualitative conclusion from the baseline model survives richer dynamics.

The October-2026 results reinforce the distinction between two claims. The first concerns whether a simple constant-volatility model prices WTI APOs accurately in absolute terms. The negative pooled mean errors in Section~\ref{sec:results} indicate nontrivial model-to-market discrepancies. The second concerns whether integrating parameter uncertainty improves on using point estimates \emph{within the same model}. The second comparison is the main contribution, and the completed baseline suggests that its incremental effect is small even when absolute fit is imperfect.

\subsection{Discounting and fixing calendars}

The current empirical baseline uses a dated U.S. Treasury par-yield curve and treats the maturity-interpolated par yield as a continuously compounded zero-rate proxy. This is transparent but approximate. A stronger robustness specification should replace it with a bootstrapped nominal zero curve or SOFR/OIS discount factors without changing the rest of the pricing pipeline.

The October-2026 implementation uses an explicit CL last-trade-date table for the first-nearby roll but a weekday fixing schedule for the averaging month. The selected month contains no full-day CME energy closure, so this approximation does not create an obvious missing full-day fixing in the pilot. Broader expiry coverage should nevertheless use an explicit CME energy settlement calendar, including abbreviated sessions where relevant, before publication-level cross-maturity comparisons are interpreted.

\subsection{Monte Carlo accuracy}

All competing pricing rules are evaluated with common random numbers and large path counts so that pairwise differences are not dominated by simulation noise. Selected contracts should be repriced at substantially larger simulation budgets to verify convergence of $\Delta_{FB,PM}$ at the sub-cent scale observed in the strict positive-volume sample. Where a geometric control variate is not directly available for the exact first-nearby structure, the variance-reduction method should be documented separately from the standard Asian benchmark rather than applying an inappropriate closed form.

\subsection{Sample composition and external scope}

The current contract-consistent empirical evidence is concentrated in the October-2026 expiry because the committed Barchart CL histories provide the required contemporaneous curve for that panel. The broader option database spans eight expiry months, but equivalent pricing panels require matching contract-specific CL histories and validated fixing calendars for each expiry. Extending those inputs is the next empirical scope expansion.

Calls and puts should continue to be reported jointly and separately when sample sizes permit. Because the downloaded panel was intentionally not chosen only by current open interest, future analysis should also verify that results are not driven by contracts that became economically irrelevant after sharp changes in WTI prices. The main interpretation should remain conditional on observed market participation rather than treating every end-of-day mark as equally informative.
