\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Parameter uncertainty arises because option-pricing models use parameters estimated from finite samples. Bayesian approaches can integrate conditional option values over a parameter posterior instead of substituting point estimates. Related work addresses implied-volatility forecasting \cite{guidolin2003}, risk-neutral predictive densities \cite{romboutsstentoft2014}, and calibration of exotic derivatives \cite{guptareisinger2014}. We examine when integrating over the historical-volatility posterior materially changes WTI Average Price Option (APO) values.

For arithmetic-average Asian options, volatility sensitivity varies with moneyness, maturity, and the share of the average already fixed. Geometric-average methods and conditioning approximations provide established numerical approaches \cite{kemnavorst1990,curran1994}, while commodity models allow richer futures dynamics \cite{schwartz1997,ewaldwu2023}. In WTI, \cite{shirayatakahashi2011} calibrate risk-neutral models to vanilla options before valuing average-price contracts, and \cite{ganwangyang2020} study APO pricing with machine learning. We retain a one-factor pricing model to separate two questions: the effect of posterior integration and the effect of using historical rather than option-implied volatility.

Historical returns identify the volatility posterior under the physical measure; conditional option values are computed under the risk-neutral measure. Let \(C^Q(\sigma)\) denote the risk-neutral APO value conditional on volatility \(\sigma\). The posterior-integrated and posterior-mean rules are
\begin{equation}
 C_{PI}=\mathbb E[C^Q(\sigma)\mid\mathcal D],
 \qquad
 C_{PM}=C^Q(\mathbb E[\sigma\mid\mathcal D]).
 \label{eq:intro_rules}
\end{equation}
Nonlinearity in volatility implies that PI and PM need not coincide. A second-order expansion gives
\begin{equation}
 C_{PI}-C_{PM}
 \approx
 \frac12 C^{Q\prime\prime}(\bar\sigma)
 \operatorname{Var}(\sigma\mid\mathcal D),
 \qquad
 \bar\sigma=\mathbb E[\sigma\mid\mathcal D].
 \label{eq:intro_taylor}
\end{equation}
The approximation links the PI--PM difference to posterior variance and curvature with respect to volatility. By contrast, the posterior standard deviation of conditional model prices is locally first order in the posterior standard deviation of volatility. Thus, PI and PM can be close while parameter uncertainty still generates substantial dispersion in model prices.

The empirical application uses CME WTI APOs based on the calendar-month arithmetic average of first-nearby futures settlements. Pricing incorporates realized fixings, the remaining fixing schedule, and the contemporaneous futures curve. We conduct three tests: a synthetic study of the PI--PM difference; out-of-sample APO pricing with historical versus earlier-date APO-implied volatility; and a cross-instrument comparison using implied volatility from standard monthly WTI futures options.

Implied volatility can improve pricing fit even under a misspecified model. Forward-looking crude-oil option information has been shown to contain predictive volatility content beyond historical models \cite{gildertsiaras2020}, and flexible implied-volatility specifications can fit the contemporaneous cross-section while still deteriorating out of sample \cite{dumasflemingwhaley1998}. More generally, richer option models can improve some performance criteria without dominating on all dimensions \cite{bakshicaochen1997,cumminsesposito2025}. Accordingly, we interpret implied volatility as a model-dependent pricing input, not a uniquely identified risk-neutral diffusion coefficient.

Three findings emerge. The synthetic study supports Equation~\eqref{eq:intro_taylor} and shows that PI and PM can diverge in short-sample, high-curvature settings. In the observed October-2026 APO sample, their difference is negligible relative to posterior model-price dispersion. Finally, earlier option-implied volatility substantially outperforms the tested historical inputs in out-of-sample APO pricing, including rolling-window, EWMA, and horizon-matched GARCH estimates. This advantage survives a same-contract volatility-persistence benchmark and transfers to implied volatility inferred from standard monthly WTI options.

The pricing-error comparison does not identify a structural physical-versus-risk-neutral volatility difference. Model-implied volatility may incorporate risk premia, omitted dynamics, liquidity, and settlement conventions. Claims are restricted to the data and specifications tested.
