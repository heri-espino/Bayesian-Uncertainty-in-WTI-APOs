\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We study posterior integration and volatility-input choice in WTI APO valuation. A second-order approximation relates the PI--PM difference to posterior volatility variance and price curvature. The synthetic experiment shows that this difference can be substantial in short-sample, high-curvature settings.

In the October-2026 sample, PI and PM are nearly identical although the posterior distribution of conditional model prices remains considerably wider. The effect of posterior integration on a point price must therefore be distinguished from parameter-induced price dispersion.

Earlier-date option-implied volatility delivers substantially lower out-of-sample pricing errors than the tested historical-volatility benchmarks, including rolling windows, EWMA, and horizon-matched GARCH. The advantage persists with same-contract lagged implied volatility and in the cross-instrument LO comparison. This empirical result does not identify a structural difference between physical and risk-neutral volatility.

Within the maintained model, volatility-input choice matters far more for pricing accuracy than the PI--PM distinction. The evidence is nevertheless limited by relatively few valuation dates, uneven liquidity, and the one-factor specification.
