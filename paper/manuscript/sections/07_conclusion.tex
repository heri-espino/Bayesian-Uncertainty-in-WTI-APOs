\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We examine whether averaging over the volatility posterior changes WTI Average Price Option values. A second-order approximation relates the PI--PM difference to posterior variance and price curvature. In synthetic data, the difference can be large when the return history is short and curvature is strong.

In the October-2026 WTI sample, PI and PM are nearly identical. Yet parameter uncertainty produces a much wider range of model prices. A small \(C_{PI}-C_{PM}\) is therefore not evidence that volatility is known precisely.

The source of the volatility estimate matters much more for observed pricing errors. Volatility estimated from earlier option prices outperforms the tested historical methods, including rolling windows, EWMA, and GARCH. This does not require fitting an implied-volatility smile and also holds when volatility comes from standard monthly WTI futures options. It does not identify a structural difference between physical and risk-neutral volatility.

We do not propose a new pricing model. Within the chosen model, using earlier option-implied volatility reduces pricing errors much more than replacing PM with PI. The evidence is limited by few market dates, uneven liquidity, and the one-factor pricing assumption.
