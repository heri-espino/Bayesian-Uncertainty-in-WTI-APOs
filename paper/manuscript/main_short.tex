\abstract[Abstract]{
We examine whether integrating uncertainty about historical volatility materially changes WTI Average Price Option (APO) values, and compare its effect with the choice of volatility input. Posterior-integrated pricing (PI) averages conditional values over the volatility posterior, whereas posterior-mean plug-in pricing (PM) uses posterior mean volatility. A second-order approximation links their difference to posterior variance and price curvature. In 175,000 synthetic return histories, PI and PM can differ substantially when estimation samples are short and price curvature is pronounced. In the October-2026 WTI sample, however, their mean absolute difference is only \$0.000745 per barrel, compared with a mean width of \$0.2733 for the central 95\% posterior interval of conditional model prices. On 2,381 out-of-sample APO observations, historical-volatility PI has MAE 2.6187, compared with 0.1075 for a previous-day APO implied-volatility smile. Rolling-window, EWMA, and horizon-matched GARCH benchmarks do not achieve similar pricing errors. The option-implied advantage persists without smile fitting and when implied volatility is inferred from standard monthly WTI futures options. The evidence is conditional on the maintained one-factor pricing model and the observed sample.
}

\bmsection*{Data availability}
The WTI APO and CL histories used in the empirical application were obtained through a commercial Barchart subscription. The independent vanilla-WTI validation uses CME/NYMEX LO option and CL futures statistics accessed through Databento. Raw proprietary market-data files are not redistributed. The public repository provides code, query metadata, schemas, provenance records, diagnostics, hashes, and derived results consistent with the applicable data licenses.

\bmsection*{Code availability}

The public repository is \url{https://github.com/heri-espino/Bayesian-Uncertainty-in-WTI-APOs}.
