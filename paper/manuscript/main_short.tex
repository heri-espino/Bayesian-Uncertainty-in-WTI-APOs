\title{When Does Posterior Integration Change Option Values? Evidence from WTI Average Price Options}

\author[1]{Heriberto Espino Montelongo}
\authormark{ESPINO MONTELONGO}
\titlemark{POSTERIOR INTEGRATION IN WTI AVERAGE PRICE OPTIONS}

\address[1]{\orgname{Universidad de las Am\'ericas Puebla}, \orgaddress{\state{Puebla}, \country{Mexico}}}
\corres{Corresponding author: Heriberto Espino Montelongo. \email{[corresponding email]}}

\abstract[Abstract]{
Volatility must be estimated, so option prices are uncertain. We examine two questions for WTI Average Price Options. First, does averaging option values over a volatility posterior give a different price from using posterior mean volatility? Second, which volatility inputs produce lower errors against market settlements? A second-order approximation shows how the first difference depends on posterior variance and the curvature of the option price. In 175,000 synthetic return histories, the difference can be substantial when the historical sample is short. In the October-2026 WTI sample, however, the mean absolute difference is only \$0.000745 per barrel. The mean width of the 95\% posterior interval for the model price is much larger, at \$0.2733 per barrel. Thus, a small change in the mean price does not imply little parameter uncertainty. On 2,381 out-of-sample observations, implied volatility estimated from earlier option prices gives much lower pricing errors than the historical-volatility methods tested, including rolling windows, EWMA, and GARCH. The improvement remains without fitting a volatility smile and when volatility comes from standard monthly WTI futures options. These results concern the tested sample and a fixed one-factor pricing model.
}

\bmsection*{Data availability}
The WTI APO and CL histories used in the empirical application were obtained through a commercial Barchart subscription. The independent vanilla-WTI validation uses CME/NYMEX LO option and CL futures statistics accessed through Databento. Raw proprietary market-data files are not redistributed. The public repository provides code, query metadata, schemas, provenance records, diagnostics, hashes, and derived results consistent with the applicable data licenses.

\bmsection*{Code availability}
