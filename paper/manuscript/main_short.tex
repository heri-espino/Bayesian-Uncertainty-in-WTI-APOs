\titlemark{VOLATILITY UNCERTAINTY IN WTI APOS}

\abstract[Abstract]{
We ask whether accounting for volatility parameter uncertainty materially changes WTI Average Price Option (APO) prices, and whether that effect is more important than the volatility information used for valuation. Posterior-integrated pricing (PI) averages option values over the volatility posterior; posterior-mean plug-in pricing (PM) uses its mean. We relate their difference to posterior variance and option-price curvature and examine the mechanism in 10 million synthetic return histories. For 310 October-2026-expiry contract-date observations, the mean absolute PI--PM difference is \$0.000745 per barrel, despite a mean 95\% posterior model-price interval width of \$0.2733. In a separate date-ordered conditional pricing comparison involving 2,381 option-date observations on only 15 valuation dates, earlier option-implied volatility produces lower errors against end-of-day settlement-price proxies than the tested historical-volatility estimates (MAE \$0.1075 versus \$2.6187 per barrel). The result also holds on the matched same-contract implied-volatility benchmark, without fitting a smile. Our comparisons are conditional on contemporaneous futures inputs, a one-factor pricing model, sparse trading, and unverified vendor publication times. A small integration correction should not be interpreted as evidence of small parameter-induced price uncertainty or overall pricing accuracy. A bounded, pilot-informed synthetic stress test illustrates this distinction under hypothetical correlation misspecification; its synthetic interval-coverage frequencies are stable across four volatility grids, but this does not establish market calibration.
}

\bmsection*{Plain language summary}
For WTI crude-oil average-price options, allowing for uncertainty about estimated volatility may change a model price only slightly. That does not imply the price is accurate or that volatility uncertainty is unimportant. We compare two ways to use the same volatility posterior and examine how much the choice of historical or earlier option-implied volatility affects conditional pricing errors.
