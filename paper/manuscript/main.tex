\title{When Does Bayesian Parameter Uncertainty Matter? Evidence from WTI Average Price Options}

\author[1]{Heriberto Espino Montelongo}
\authormark{ESPINO MONTELONGO}
\titlemark{BAYESIAN UNCERTAINTY IN WTI AVERAGE PRICE OPTIONS}

\corres{Corresponding author: Heriberto Espino Montelongo. \email{[corresponding email]}}

\abstract[Abstract]{
This paper asks when Bayesian uncertainty about historically estimated volatility materially changes WTI Average Price Option values, and how that effect compares with using a volatility state informed by option markets. Statistical learning is conducted under the physical measure and derivative valuation under the risk-neutral measure, so physical drift uncertainty is not treated as pricing uncertainty. A 175,000-history synthetic mechanism map shows that posterior-integrated and posterior-mean plug-in prices are usually close, but the largest observed absolute gap reaches 0.1384 when historical information is scarce and the pricing map is highly curved; the second-order term $\tfrac12 C^{Q\prime\prime}(\bar\sigma)\operatorname{Var}(\sigma\mid\mathcal D)$ closely tracks this behavior. In the contract-consistent October-2026 WTI panel, posterior-integrated and posterior-mean RMSE are 0.5177 and 0.5183. High-precision pseudo-Monte Carlo, randomized Sobol, and Curran benchmarks confirm that the sub-cent integration gaps are numerically resolved. The larger empirical effect comes from volatility specification. A strict forward-in-time APO-implied-volatility experiment spans seven expiries and 2,381 holdouts: historical-volatility posterior integration has RMSE 3.4090, compared with 0.1725 for an expanding prior-date smile and 0.1594 for a previous-day smile. To test whether this improvement is merely endogenous to the APO family, an independent vanilla-WTI validation constructs American-option implied volatilities from prior-date CME LO settlements. On 253 October-2026 contract-dates lying inside the observed LO moneyness support, historical-volatility RMSE is 0.5562, versus 0.2599 for the external previous-day LO surface and 0.2686 for the corresponding prior-date APO smile. Valuation-date cluster bootstraps confirm a large external-LO improvement over historical volatility, while LO and APO surfaces are broadly comparable. Posterior integration is therefore a second-order refinement in the observed WTI application, whereas the volatility state used by the pricing model is first-order; in weak-information, high-curvature regimes, that ordering can reverse.
}

\bmsection*{Data availability}
The APO and CL histories used in the empirical application were obtained through a commercial Barchart subscription. The independent vanilla-WTI validation uses CME/NYMEX LO option and CL futures statistics accessed through Databento. Redistribution of raw proprietary market-data files is not assumed to be permitted. The public repository therefore prioritizes code, query metadata, schemas, provenance records, diagnostics, hashes, and derived results consistent with the applicable data licenses.

\bmsection*{Code availability}
The research code is organized as an installable Python package, \texttt{bayesian\_asian\_options}, with documented APIs, tests, deterministic seeds, and reproducible experiment entry points. A versioned code snapshot will accompany the final article.
