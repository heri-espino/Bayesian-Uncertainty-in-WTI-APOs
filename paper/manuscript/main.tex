\abstract[Abstract]{
Parameter uncertainty is often treated as secondary in derivative valuation, even though volatility is estimated from finite and potentially unstable samples. This paper develops a Bayesian framework that separates statistical learning under the physical measure from no-arbitrage valuation under the risk-neutral measure and propagates posterior uncertainty in volatility into arithmetic-average option prices. The framework is evaluated first in repeated-sampling experiments, where the true risk-neutral price is known, and then in an empirical application to CME WTI Average Price Options. The empirical database contains 213 unique call and put contracts and 11,179 option-date observations spanning expiries from September 2026 to June 2029. A first contract-consistent October-2026 panel contains 310 option-date observations across 12 eligible valuation dates. At the production computational budget, full posterior integration and posterior-mean plug-in pricing remain nearly indistinguishable: pooled RMSE is 0.5177 versus 0.5183 in the full October panel and 0.1864 versus 0.1869 in the six contract-date observations with positive reported volume. The evidence therefore indicates that, within the constant-volatility one-factor baseline, posterior integration adds little to the point price relative to pricing at posterior mean volatility, while overall model-to-market discrepancies remain much larger. The strict positive-volume sample is small, so the empirical result is interpreted as a baseline bound on the incremental value of posterior integration rather than as a universal ranking of pricing rules.
}
